Who needs this

Three reasons an array comes down.

In every case the roof is the job and the array is the obstacle.

A re-roof under an existing array

The most common one. The roof has reached the end of its life while the panels are barely halfway through theirs.

A leak or repair beneath panels

Water finding its way in under or near the array means modules have to move before anyone can chase the source properly.

Storm or structural work

Decking damage, a failed section, or framing that needs attention. Panels get removed carefully, not levered off in a hurry.

Sun Worx installation

What is included

Off, stored, back on, verified.

The difference between a careful removal and a rushed one shows up in what still works afterward.

Labelled before anything moves

Every module, conductor and connector is tagged and photographed in place so the array goes back exactly as engineered.

Tested coming down

We record production per module before removal, which gives us a baseline to prove against when the array is back up.

Careful removal

Modules, rail and mounts come off by hand, in order, without stressing frames, conductors or connectors.

Stored on site or off

Panels are stacked, protected and kept dry for as long as the roofing work needs. They are not left leaning against a fence.

Re-flashed penetrations

Every mount goes back into new flashing on the new surface. Old holes are closed properly as part of the roofing work.

Verified before we leave

Reconnected, powered up, and checked module by module against the baseline. If a panel underperforms we find it now, not in July.

How it works

Six steps, one array, no surprises.

Coordinated with the roofing schedule so the roof is never open longer than it has to be.

  1. 01

    System check and documentation

    Production data, layout photos, equipment list and connector types recorded before a single bolt is touched.

  2. 02

    Shutdown and isolation

    The system is powered down and isolated properly, with disconnects verified rather than assumed.

  3. 03

    Removal

    Modules first, then rail, then mounts, labelled as they come off and staged where the roofing crew is not working.

  4. 04

    Storage while the roof is done

    Panels stay protected and dry on site, or leave with us if the yard cannot accommodate them safely.

  5. 05

    Reinstall on new flashing

    Mounts set into fresh flashing, rail squared, modules back in their original positions and torqued to spec.

  6. 06

    Recommission and compare

    System back online, then production checked against the pre-removal baseline and the findings handed to you in writing.

Money and rules

Why this is a sequencing decision.

Removal and reinstall is roofing-driven work, so the energy programs do not apply to it. The practical point is timing: a roof replaced before an array goes up never needs this service at all, which is why we raise roof condition during solar design. If your roof has under a decade left and panels are already up there, doing the roof now and resetting the array once is the last time either trade has to touch it for years.

If the array is old enough that reinstalling it makes little sense, we will say that too, and show you the comparison rather than quietly quoting the bigger job.

Questions

Removal and reinstall questions.

How long is my system off?+

Usually as long as the roofing takes, which is a few days on most houses. We schedule removal and reinstall around the roofing crew so there is no idle week in the middle.

Will my panels still be under warranty afterwards?+

Manufacturer coverage stays with the equipment. What matters is that handling is documented and careful, which is why we photograph and label everything.

Do you work on systems you did not install?+

Yes. Most of this work is on arrays other companies built. We record what is there and put it back the way it was engineered.

What if a panel fails during the process?+

The pre-removal baseline tells us whether it was already failing. Either way you get the data and a straight recommendation.

Can the roof be done without removing the panels?+

Only for work well clear of the array. Anything under or immediately around the modules needs them off to do the flashing properly.
