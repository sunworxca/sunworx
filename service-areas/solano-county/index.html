PG&E, and MCE in two cities

Who sells you power in Solano

PG&E owns the wires county-wide, and two cities buy their electricity through MCE instead.

PG&E across the county

Vacaville, Dixon and Suisun City homes are on PG&E for both the power and the delivery, and PG&E handles interconnection everywhere.

MCE in Fairfield and Vallejo

MCE, a public agency, purchases electricity for these two cities while PG&E delivers it, and MCE offers a monthly credit for batteries that support the grid.

Rates you can plan around

What helps here

Solano County incentives

For Fairfield and Vallejo households served by MCE, the agency has offered a monthly bill credit of roughly $10 to $20 for batteries enrolled in its grid-support program, which adds up quietly over the years. Everywhere in the county, SGIP remains the main path to significant storage funding for households that qualify on medical need or fire-risk location. The federal 30% credit for purchased home solar ended December 31, 2025, and some third-party-owned lease and PPA programs can still apply a federal credit through 2027.

Credit amounts, enrollment rules and program availability are set by the agencies and can change, and some require your battery to respond to grid events. We confirm what your address and equipment actually qualify for before you commit.

Common questions

Solano County questions

Who is MCE and why is it on my Fairfield bill?+

MCE is a public agency that buys electricity for its member cities while PG&E delivers it. For battery owners, MCE has offered a monthly bill credit for letting storage support the grid.

I am in Vacaville. Do I get the MCE credit?+

Not currently. Vacaville is served on PG&E terms, so we look at SGIP and at how storage shifts your own usage instead of an MCE credit.

Does Delta wind affect how you mount panels?+

It affects attachment spacing and hardware choice, which we account for in the engineering. It is a design detail, not an obstacle.

My Vallejo roof is steep and old. Can you still work on it?+

Usually yes, with the right safety setup. If the roof is near the end of its life we will say so and price a re-roof option rather than mounting on borrowed time.
